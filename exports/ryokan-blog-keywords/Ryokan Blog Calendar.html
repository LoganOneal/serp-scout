How often to post

Google doesn't reward a posting schedule. It ranks pages that answer the search better than what's already there. Frequency only matters because it decides how much good work gets done.

  • One a day is the most worth publishing. More than that gives no ranking benefit, and each post gets less internal linking and promotion.
  • 2 a week is the sustainable default for a newer site where each post needs real research, fact checks against the site's own pages, and Kai's review.
  • Don't bulk-publish. Dozens of similar AI-assisted pages at once is the pattern Google's scaled-content spam policy targets. Steady, distinct, useful posts are safe.
  • Consistency beats bursts. A steady pace gets new pages crawled sooner and lets you see which topics rank before writing more of them.

How to read the numbers

  • Google: Google Ads Keyword Planner, US, 12-month average. It groups close variants, so "onsen japan" and "japanese onsen" show the same number.
  • Semrush: exact-phrase US volume. Use it to choose the spelling for the title and H1.
  • KD: Semrush difficulty, 0 to 100. Under 20 is easy, 20s possible, 30s harder, 40 and up hard for a new site.
  • Peak: the month with the most Google searches. New posts usually take 2 to 6 months to rank, so publish a few months before the peak.
  • US only. UK, Canada and Australia add roughly 40 to 60% more.
