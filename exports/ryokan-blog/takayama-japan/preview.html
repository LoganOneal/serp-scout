Takayama, Japan: How Many Days, What to Do and When to Go

Published on November 23, 2026.

By Kai Sulkin, founder of Ryokan Society

Takayama, Japan is an old merchant town in the Hida mountains of Gifu Prefecture, often called Hida-Takayama. It is known for streets of dark wooden houses, two morning markets, sake breweries more than a century old and the Takayama Festival, held each spring and autumn. It's well worth a night, and two if you add Shirakawa-go or the Okuhida hot springs. We list 24 ryokan in Takayama, along with 13 onsen hotels, 4 machiya and 3 minshuku, and most cost $90 to $460 a night for two.

Our guide details how many days to spend in Takayama, transportation from Tokyo, Kyoto, and Nagoya, activities, the best visiting times, and lodging for each kind of trip. We confirmed pricing, operating hours, and bus schedules on November 23, 2026.

What is Takayama?

Takayama is a small city in the Hida region of northern Gifu, in the mountains between Nagoya and the Sea of Japan coast. In the feudal era it supplied timber and skilled carpenters, and the shogunate ruled it directly. That money paid for the merchant houses you see today and for the festival floats, which are among the most elaborate in Japan.

The historic Sanmachi district, located east of the station across the Miyagawa River, attracts most tourists. Its three parallel streets are lined with two-story wooden buildings containing sake breweries, shops selling miso and pickles, cafes, and compact museums. Most attractions are a short stroll from JR Takayama Station, meaning a car or bus pass is unnecessary for exploring.

The name "Hida-Takayama" refers to the same location. It's used by locals and on schedules to distinguish this city from other places in Japan named Takayama.

Is Takayama worth visiting?

It certainly is. While Kyoto possesses more temples and Kanazawa a larger garden, very few central Japanese towns preserve an entire merchant district so completely. Takayama also provides two daily morning markets, an active festival culture, and the mountains on its doorstep.

A common concern is whether it feels overly crowded. The historic area gets busy from late morning until mid-afternoon when tour groups visiting Shirakawa-go pass through. Overnight guests experience the quieter periods: the morning markets before buses come and the calm streets after shops shut.

How many days in Takayama?

One night lets you see the town. Get there in the early afternoon, explore Sanmachi and the riverbank, then visit the morning market and Takayama Jinya the next morning before you go.

Two nights give you time for an excursion. You can reach Shirakawa-go by bus in around 50 minutes. Buses from Takayama also go to the Okuhida hot-spring villages and the alpine valley of Kamikochi. To stay in the mountains, reserve your first night in Takayama and your second at a ryokan in Okuhida, where we list 33 ryokan with nightly rates for two from about $160 to $500.

A day trip from Tokyo is too much travel for too little time. The journey from Tokyo takes around 4.5 hours one way, meaning nine hours of transit for just four or five hours in Takayama. Starting from Nagoya, with a trip of about 2 hours 30 minutes each direction, a day visit is possible, though you would not experience the morning market.

For a single overnight stay, pick a place close to the station. Oyado Koto No Yume is just a 2-minute walk away, and Ryokan Kaminaka is a 3-minute walk, so you can leave your luggage and reach the old town quickly.

How to get to Takayama

Takayama Station lies along the JR Takayama Line. The Hida limited express from Nagoya is how most visitors get here; long-distance buses arrive at the nearby Nohi Bus Center.

Things to do in Takayama

Walk Sanmachi, the old town

Begin your walk at Nakabashi Bridge, the red one crossing the Miyagawa River, heading east into the historic district. Visiting early or late helps avoid crowds in your pictures. Watch for the sugidama above brewery doors, a sphere fashioned from cedar boughs. It is hung green to mark the new sake's completion, then browns while the sake ages.

The morning markets

The town hosts two separate morning markets. You can find the Miyagawa market along the Miyagawa River's edge, stretching from Kajibashi Bridge to Yayoi Bridge. The smaller Jinya-mae market sits in the square directly facing Takayama Jinya. Each market provides vegetables, pickles, miso, flowers, and crafts.

The Miyagawa market runs from 7:00 between April and November, changing to an 8:00 start from December through March, and it closes around noon. Arrive early, because stalls begin closing once their goods are sold, and you will find fewer vendors on rainy, snowy, or winter days.

Takayama Jinya

Takayama Jinya is the Edo-period government office from which the shogunate's officials ran the Hida region. You walk through tatami rooms, offices and storehouses. Admission is 500 yen, and free for anyone 18 or younger. It opens at 8:45 and closes at 17:00 from April to October and at 16:30 from November to March; the ticket window closes 30 minutes earlier. It's closed December 29 to January 3.

Shoes are removed at the entryway and you carry them in a bag. Slippers are not available to use in winter, and since the building has no heating, wearing warm socks is a good idea.

The festival floats, even out of season

Even if you miss the festival, you can view a changing display of the autumn festival's floats at the Takayama Festival Float Exhibition Hall. This hall is at Sakurayama Hachimangu, the shrine located at the north end of the old town, and it operates year-round.

Hida Folk Village

Hida no Sato is an outdoor museum featuring over 30 traditional farmhouses and storehouses from the Hida area, with most constructed during the Edo period. Admission runs about 700 yen, and it's a 10-minute bus ride from the station. For visitors who won't make it to Shirakawa-go, this museum is the next best thing.

Sake tasting

You'll find seven sake breweries in Takayama, each operating for over 100 years. Most sit in Sanmachi, and a few let you taste at the counter.

The Higashiyama walking course

A walking course approximately 3.5 km long runs along the hill east of the old town. It connects a series of temples and shrines and goes past the site of the old Takayama castle. Plan for about two hours; it offers a peaceful break from the busy Sanmachi area.

When to visit Takayama

SeasonWhat's on
Spring (March to May)The spring Takayama Festival, Sanno Matsuri, occurs April 14-15. Twelve floats proceed from Hie Shrine in the old town's southern area.
Summer (June to August)Morning markets are busiest. Hand-held fireworks along the Miyagawa in August.
Autumn (September to November)Hachiman Matsuri, the autumn Takayama Festival, October 9 and 10. Eleven floats originate at Sakurayama Hachimangu in the north. The hills display autumn colors.
Winter (December to February)The old town has snow and fewer tourists. The market starts an hour later, and the Jinya lacks heating.

Both festivals occur on identical dates annually. UNESCO inscribed them together in 2016 as part of Japan's float festivals. When festivals are on, lodgings close to the old town fill fast, so secure your booking once your plans are set.

I prefer visiting in autumn. The hills above the town change color, and the shrine steps surrounding the old town look their best.

Takayama food guide

The local specialty visitors seek is Hida beef, a marbled meat from cattle raised in this area. You can find it prepared as steak, grilled on a hoba magnolia leaf with miso, or served as sushi from old-town street stalls. Also sample Takayama ramen, which has a thin soy-based broth and curly noodles, and mitarashi dango, grilled rice dumplings coated in a soy glaze.

Many inns near the station don't serve dinner. Oyado Koto No Yume sells only room-only and breakfast plans, and Ichinomatsu gives you a breakfast box. If you want a full dinner at your inn, choose one that serves it, such as Oyado Hachibei or Honjin Hiranoya Bekkan.

I grew up in Tokyo in a hotel family, and one thing I learned early is that a breakfast-only plan isn't a lesser stay. In a town like Takayama, where the evening is quiet and the restaurants close early, a breakfast-only inn makes sense if you've booked dinner at a Hida beef restaurant. If you haven't, book the dinner plan. An inn dinner is the easiest good meal you'll have in Takayama, and you won't have to go looking for an open restaurant at 8 pm.

Does Takayama have onsen? Private baths and tattoos

Takayama isn't a bathhouse town like Kinosaki or Kusatsu, but several inns pipe in hot-spring water, sold as Hida Takayama Onsen. The water varies by inn: Wanosato has sulfur-containing water that flows straight from the source into its large bath, while Hoshokaku and Honjin Hiranoya Bekkan have a simple spring. For a full onsen town, go up into the mountains to Okuhida.

For visitors who prefer not to bathe with others, Takayama offers more private bathing choices than most towns its size. This count includes onsen hotels, and we list 4 places to stay with a bath in the room and 15 with a private bath you book for your party alone. At Ryokan Murayama, two hot-spring baths for private use are complimentary, and Hidatei Hanaougi provides rooms with their own open-air bath.

On tattoos, most Takayama inns don't say anything in writing. A few say outright that tattooed guests can only use the private baths, including Wat Hotel & Spa Hida Takayama and Hida Hanasatonoyu Takayama Ouan. Our filter shows the 15 places where you can bathe privately with tattoos.

My method for inquiring about tattoo policies avoids asking if they are permitted. Instead, I ask if the kashikiri-buro, or private bath, is available for booking and at what time. This question has a simple yes or no answer, does not make the inn uncomfortable, and gets you into the hot water regardless. The full etiquette is covered in our guide to onsen in Japan.

Wanosato holds One Michelin Key for 2026 and sits outside the town center, with a shuttle from the west exit of Takayama Station at 14:20, 15:20 and 16:20. Machiya and minshuku

Takayama also has 4 machiya and 3 minshuku on our list. A machiya is an old wooden townhouse rented whole, with tatami rooms and often a small inner garden. A minshuku is a family-run guesthouse with a few rooms and home cooking. A machiya suits a family or a group who want to cook and come and go as they like. A ryokan is the better choice if you want dinner served and the bath drawn for you.

Day excursions from Takayama

Shirakawa-go. The village of steep thatched gassho farmhouses is about 50 minutes away by Nohi Bus, and the bus must be reserved. Many people stay the night to see it after the day visitors leave: Onyado Yuinosho has a private bath to reserve and shared hot-spring baths, though tattoos aren't allowed in the shared ones.

Hida-Furukawa. This is a more compact and peaceful historic town located a brief train journey north of Takayama on the JR line. Yatsusankan there holds a rating of 9.8 out of 10.

Okuhida Onsen and Kamikochi. The Okuhida area consists of several hot-spring villages situated in the mountains to the east of Takayama, and you access them via Nohi Bus. Continuing from Hirayu to Kamikochi, a mountain valley that shuts down each winter, takes approximately 30 minutes. Maintenance work will close the Shinhotaka Ropeway from November 24 through November 27, 2026.

Takayama FAQ

Is there snowfall in Takayama? It is. Snow falls from December through February, and the sight of snow on the old town's dark wooden houses is among Japan's finest. Road and bus service can be disrupted, so review alerts from the local tourism office and Nohi Bus when planning a day trip. See Takayama ryokan for winter dates →

What are the dates for the Takayama Festival in 2026 and 2027? The Sanno Matsuri spring festival occurs April 14 and 15, with the Hachiman Matsuri autumn festival on October 9 and 10. These dates do not change from year to year. Accommodations close to the old town are reserved far ahead, so you need to make your booking early.

Are there onsen in Takayama? Several inns offer one, but you can't stroll between separate public bathhouses like in Kinosaki. To arrange a private bath, review the 15 places with a private bath to book. For a nearby classic onsen town, consider Okuhida or Gero Onsen.

Is Takayama suitable for a day trip? You can get there from Nagoya in around 2 hours and 30 minutes each way via limited express train. Coming from Tokyo, Kyoto, or Osaka isn't feasible for a single day, and you wouldn't see the morning market. Plan to stay overnight.

What is the price of a ryokan in Takayama? For two people, most ryokan we list run from $90 to $460 per night, with a typical price near $190. To view each inn's price, enter your dates on our Takayama page.

Is Takayama overly touristy? The historic district gets very busy during midday. It's much quieter in the early morning and evening, and that is the main advantage of an overnight stay compared to a tour visit.
