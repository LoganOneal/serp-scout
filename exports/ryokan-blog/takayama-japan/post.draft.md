It is known for streets of dark wooden houses, two morning markets, sake breweries more than a century old and the Takayama Festival, held each spring and autumn. It's well worth a night, and two if you add Shirakawa-go or the Okuhida hot springs. ## What is Takayama?

Takayama is a small city in the Hida region of northern Gifu, in the mountains between Nagoya and the Sea of Japan coast. In the feudal era it supplied timber and skilled carpenters, and the shogunate ruled it directly. That money paid for the merchant houses you see today and for the festival floats, which are among the most elaborate in Japan.

Most visitors spend their time in Sanmachi, the old merchant quarter east of the station across the Miyagawa River. Three parallel lanes of two-story wooden houses hold sake breweries, miso and pickle shops, cafes and small museums. Most sights are within walking distance of JR Takayama Station, so you don't need a car or a bus pass to see the town.

The name "Hida-Takayama" is the same place. Locals and timetables use it to tell the city apart from other Takayamas in Japan.

## Is Takayama worth visiting?

Yes. Kyoto has more temples and Kanazawa has a bigger garden, but few towns in central Japan keep a whole merchant district this intact, and Takayama adds two daily morning markets, a living festival tradition and the mountains on its doorstep.

People ask whether it's too touristy. The old town is busy from late morning to mid-afternoon, when tour groups come through on their way to Shirakawa-go. Stay the night and you get the other hours: the morning markets before the buses arrive, and quiet lanes after the shops close.

## How many days in Takayama?

**One night** covers the town: arrive by early afternoon, walk Sanmachi and the riverside, and do the morning market and Takayama Jinya the next day before you leave.

**Two nights** let you add a day trip. Shirakawa-go is about 50 minutes away by bus, and the Okuhida hot-spring villages and the alpine valley of Kamikochi are reached by bus from Takayama. If you'd rather sleep in the mountains, spend the first night in Takayama and the second at a ryokan in [Okuhida](https://ryokansociety.com/okuhida), where we list 33 ryokan from about $160 to $500 a night for two.

**A day trip from Tokyo isn't worth it.** Takayama is about 4.5 hours from Tokyo each way, so you'd spend nine hours on trains for four or five in town. From Nagoya, at about 2 hours 30 minutes each way, a day trip works, but you miss the morning market.

If you only have one night, stay near the station. [Oyado Koto No Yume](https://ryokansociety.com/takayama/oyado-koto-no-yume) is a 2-minute walk from it and [Ryokan Kaminaka](https://ryokansociety.com/takayama/ryokan-kaminaka) is a 3-minute walk, so you can drop your bags and be in the old town within minutes.

## How to get to Takayama

Takayama Station is on the JR Takayama Line. Most travelers arrive on the Hida limited express from Nagoya; the long-distance buses use the Nohi Bus Center near the station.

## Things to do in Takayama

### Walk Sanmachi, the old town

Start at the red Nakabashi Bridge over the Miyagawa and walk east into the old lanes. Go early or late in the day for photos without crowds. Look up over the doorways of the sake breweries for the sugidama, a ball of cedar branches. It goes up green when the new sake is made and turns brown as the sake matures.

### The morning markets

Takayama has two morning markets. The Miyagawa market runs along the Miyagawa River between the Kajibashi and Yayoi bridges, and the smaller Jinya-mae market is in the square in front of Takayama Jinya. Both sell vegetables, pickles, miso, flowers and crafts.

![Shoppers at the stalls of the Miyagawa morning market in Takayama](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6a/Morning_market_%2811272886764%29.jpg/1920px-Morning_market_%2811272886764%29.jpg)
*The Miyagawa morning market on the bank of the river.*

The Miyagawa market opens at 7:00 from April to November and at 8:00 from December to March, and winds down around noon. Go early: stalls start packing up once they sell out, and on rainy, snowy or winter days there are fewer of them.

### Takayama Jinya

Takayama Jinya is the Edo-period government office from which the shogunate's officials ran the Hida region. You walk through tatami rooms, offices and storehouses. Admission is 500 yen, and free for anyone 18 or younger. It opens at 8:45 and closes at 17:00 from April to October and at 16:30 from November to March; the ticket window closes 30 minutes earlier. It's closed December 29 to January 3.

You take your shoes off at the entrance and carry them in a bag. In winter there are no slippers to borrow and the building isn't heated, so bring thick socks.

### The festival floats, even out of season

If you can't be here for the festival, the Takayama Festival Float Exhibition Hall at Sakurayama Hachimangu, the shrine at the north end of the old town, shows a rotating selection of the autumn festival's floats all year.

### Hida Folk Village

Hida no Sato is an open-air museum of more than 30 old farmhouses and storehouses from the Hida region, most of them from the Edo period. It's about 10 minutes by bus from the station, and admission is about 700 yen. If you're not going to Shirakawa-go itself, this is the next best thing.

### Sake tasting

Takayama has seven sake breweries, all more than 100 years old, and most are in Sanmachi. Several offer tastings at the counter.

### The Higashiyama walking course

On the hill east of the old town, a walking course of about 3.5 km links a string of temples and shrines and passes the site of Takayama's old castle. Allow about two hours. It's a quiet change from the crowds of Sanmachi.

## When to visit Takayama

| Season | What's on |
| --- | --- |
| Spring (March to May) | The Sanno Matsuri, the spring Takayama Festival, on April 14 and 15. Twelve floats, from Hie Shrine in the south of the old town. |
| Summer (June to August) | The morning markets at their fullest. In August, hand-held fireworks on the banks of the Miyagawa. |
| Autumn (September to November) | The Hachiman Matsuri, the autumn Takayama Festival, on October 9 and 10. Eleven floats, from Sakurayama Hachimangu in the north. Autumn color on the hills. |
| Winter (December to February) | Snow on the old town and fewer visitors. The market opens an hour later, and the Jinya is unheated. |

The two festivals are held on the same dates every year. Together they were inscribed by UNESCO in 2016 as part of Japan's float festivals. On festival dates, inns near the old town fill early, so book as soon as your dates are firm.

## What to eat in Takayama

Hida beef is the dish people come for: marbled beef from cattle raised in the region, served as steak, grilled on a hoba magnolia leaf with miso, or as sushi from street stalls in the old town. Look also for Takayama ramen, a thin soy broth with curly noodles, and mitarashi dango, rice dumplings grilled with a soy glaze.

Many inns near the station don't serve dinner. [Oyado Koto No Yume](https://ryokansociety.com/takayama/oyado-koto-no-yume) sells only room-only and breakfast plans, and [Ichinomatsu](https://ryokansociety.com/takayama/ichinomatsu-japanese-modern-hotel) gives you a breakfast box. If you want a full dinner at your inn, choose one that serves it, such as [Oyado Hachibei](https://ryokansociety.com/takayama/oyado-hachibei) or [Honjin Hiranoya Bekkan](https://ryokansociety.com/takayama/honjin-hiranoya-bekkan).

I grew up in Tokyo in a hotel family, and one thing I learned early is that a breakfast-only plan isn't a lesser stay. In a town like Takayama, where the evening is quiet and the restaurants close early, a breakfast-only inn makes sense if you've booked dinner at a Hida beef restaurant. If you haven't, book the dinner plan. An inn dinner is the easiest good meal you'll have in Takayama, and you won't have to go looking for an open restaurant at 8 pm.

## Does Takayama have onsen? Private baths and tattoos

Takayama isn't a bathhouse town like [Kinosaki](https://ryokansociety.com/blog/kinosaki-onsen) or Kusatsu, but several inns pipe in hot-spring water, sold as Hida Takayama Onsen. The water varies by inn: [Wanosato](https://ryokansociety.com/takayama/wanosato) has sulfur-containing water that flows straight from the source into its large bath, while [Hoshokaku](https://ryokansociety.com/takayama/hoshokaku) and [Honjin Hiranoya Bekkan](https://ryokansociety.com/takayama/honjin-hiranoya-bekkan) have a simple spring. For a full onsen town, go up into the mountains to Okuhida.

If you'd rather not share a bath, Takayama has more private options than most towns its size. With onsen hotels included, we list [4 places to stay with a bath in the room](https://ryokansociety.com/takayama?bath=room) and [15 with a private bath you book](https://ryokansociety.com/takayama?bath=private) for your party alone. [Ryokan Murayama](https://ryokansociety.com/takayama/ryokan-murayama) has two private hot-spring baths at no extra charge, and [Hidatei Hanaougi](https://ryokansociety.com/takayama/hidatei-hanaougi) has rooms with their own open-air bath.

On tattoos, most Takayama inns don't say anything in writing. A few say outright that tattooed guests can only use the private baths, including [Wat Hotel & Spa Hida Takayama](https://ryokansociety.com/takayama/wat-hotel-spa-hida-takayama) and [Hida Hanasatonoyu Takayama Ouan](https://ryokansociety.com/takayama/hida-hanasatonoyu-takayama-ouan). Our filter shows the [15 places where you can bathe privately with tattoos](https://ryokansociety.com/takayama?tattoos=private).

When I ask an inn about tattoos, I don't ask "are tattoos allowed?" I ask whether the private bath (kashikiri-buro) can be reserved, and for what time. That question has a simple yes or no, it doesn't put the inn on the spot, and it gets you into the hot water either way. Our [guide to onsen in Japan](https://ryokansociety.com/blog/onsen-japan) covers the etiquette in full.

### Machiya and minshuku

Takayama also has 4 machiya and 3 minshuku on our list. A machiya is an old wooden townhouse rented whole, with tatami rooms and often a small inner garden. ## Day trips from Takayama

**Shirakawa-go.** The village of steep thatched gassho farmhouses is about 50 minutes away by Nohi Bus, and the bus must be reserved. Many people stay the night to see it after the day visitors leave: [Onyado Yuinosho](https://ryokansociety.com/takayama/onyado-yuinosho) has a private bath to reserve and shared hot-spring baths, though tattoos aren't allowed in the shared ones.

**Hida-Furukawa.** A smaller, quieter old town a short train ride north of Takayama on the JR line. [Yatsusankan](https://ryokansociety.com/takayama/yatsusankan) there is rated 9.8 out of 10.

**Okuhida Onsen and Kamikochi.** Okuhida is a group of hot-spring villages in the mountains east of Takayama, reached by Nohi Bus. From Hirayu it's about 30 minutes on to Kamikochi, an alpine valley that closes for the winter. The Shinhotaka Ropeway is closed for maintenance from November 24 to 27, 2026.

Many travelers don't choose: they go Nagoya to Takayama to Shirakawa-go to Kanazawa, or stop at Gero on the way up the Hida line.

## Takayama FAQ

**Does it snow in Takayama?**
Yes. December to February is the snowy season, and snow on the dark wooden houses of the old town is one of the best sights in Japan. Roads and buses can be affected, so check the tourism office's notices and [Nohi Bus](https://www.nouhibus.co.jp/english/) before a day trip. [See Takayama ryokan for winter dates →](https://ryokansociety.com/takayama)

**When is the Takayama Festival in 2026 and 2027?**
The spring festival, the Sanno Matsuri, is on April 14 and 15, and the autumn festival, the Hachiman Matsuri, is on October 9 and 10. The dates are the same every year. Inns near the old town fill early, so book as soon as you can.

**Does Takayama have onsen?**
Yes, at several inns, though there are no public bathhouses to walk between as in Kinosaki. For a private soak, see the [15 places with a private bath to book](https://ryokansociety.com/takayama?bath=private). For a classic onsen town nearby, look at [Okuhida](https://ryokansociety.com/okuhida) or [Gero Onsen](https://ryokansociety.com/gero-onsen).

**Can Takayama be a day trip?**
From Nagoya, yes, at about 2 hours 30 minutes each way by limited express. From Tokyo, Kyoto or Osaka it's too far for a day, and you'd miss the morning market. Spend at least one night.

**How much does a ryokan in Takayama cost?**
Most of the stays we list cost $90 to $460 a night for two, and the middle of the range is about $190. Set your dates on our [Takayama page](https://ryokansociety.com/takayama) to see each inn's price.

**Is Takayama too touristy?**
In the middle of the day the old town is crowded. Early morning and evening are calm, which is the best reason to stay the night rather than visit on a tour.
