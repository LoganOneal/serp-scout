## Passage 3

OLD: Onsen (温泉) means "hot spring," and in Japan the word has a legal definition. Under the Hot Springs Act, water from the ground counts as an onsen if it comes out at 25°C or warmer, or if it contains enough of at least one of 19 listed substances, such as sulfur, iron or carbonic acid. So a spring can be cool and still qualify because of what's dissolved in it, which is why some onsen baths are reheated.

NEW: The term onsen (温泉) translates to "hot spring" and has a legal meaning in Japan. According to the Hot Springs Act, groundwater qualifies if its temperature reaches 25°C or higher, or if it contains a sufficient amount of at least one of 19 listed substances, such as sulfur, iron, or carbonic acid. This means a cool spring can still be an onsen due to its mineral content, which is why some baths are reheated.
