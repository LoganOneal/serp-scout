# Guest-post feasibility and pitch writing

This skill replaces guest-post-feasibility. Use it for every new batch of leads.

Three jobs in one skill. Part A decides whether a site is worth pitching. Part B writes the pitch, or the full article when the site demands one, as Claire Bennett, the blogger behind Hotel Hot Tubs. Part C routes each piece of work. Pitch-first sites that take an email pitch are pushed to Kai's outreach tool as step 1 drafts together with a requirements note for the reviewer (sent only after Kai approves them there). Article-first sites, where the finished article is part of the first thing we send, are never pushed to the outreach tool, whether the site wants the article pasted into the email text, attached as a document, linked as a shared document, or submitted through a form: the article is written into the batch Google Doc and tracked separately in the "Full Articles" tab of the tracker, because the article itself needs its own review before anything is sent. Everything else that cannot go by email goes into the batch Google Doc. Calibrated on hand-reviewed sites, see the calibration section.

## Guiding principles

0. **Only pitch sites that clearly invite guest posts.** A site is pursued only when it has a live page or explicit statement inviting outside writers to submit guest posts or pitches (a write-for-us, contribute, submissions, contributor guidelines or guest-post page, or a clear sentence such as "we accept guest posts" on the contact or about page). A plain contact page, an advertise page, a media kit or a sponsored-post price list is not an invitation. A site with no clear invitation is not pursued (SKIP_NO_INVITATION) and is not pitched, even if it would otherwise fit. Every other criterion below still applies on top of this.
1. **Bias toward pitching, among sites that invite guest posts.** Pitching costs almost nothing, and over-pitching is not a concern. Once a site clearly invites guest posts, skip it only when there is a clear, specific reason. When on the fence about the other gates, the verdict is PURSUE and the doubt goes in `notes` and `confidence`.
2. **The site must show recent activity.** Pursue a site only when there is dated evidence of activity in the 6 months before the triage date: a dated post, a dated reply by the owner to a comment, or a dated social post linked from the site. "Last modified" metadata, sitemap lastmod values, copyright years and feed build dates do not count. Without that evidence the verdict is SKIP_INACTIVE, with the newest dated evidence found (or "none found") in the tracker's `notes` column. A site that is clearly gone (parked domain, persistent errors, no content) is still SKIP_DEAD.
3. **Fit means "can we write something their readers would want that also makes sense for us".** Subjective judgement is required here, see Gate G5. A merely imperfect fit is not a reason to skip, because an angle can usually be built from the site's requirements.
4. **Evidence over inference.** Quote the page. Label inferences. Never infer a skip from silence, with one exception: when the full Step 1 search finds no invitation to guest post, the verdict is SKIP_NO_INVITATION.
5. **Follow the site's stated rules 100%, silently, but never promise what is not true.** When a site states pitch or article criteria, every requirement is met exactly (format, length, photos, links, bio, topics, where and how to submit). Check the draft against each rule yourself. The rule quotes belong in the triage record (`evidence_quotes`, `requirements_summary`) and in the reviewer note, never in the pitch draft. The pitch does not recite the rules back to the editor, announce the process the site imposes, list deliverable specs, label sections with their limits, or mark word counts (see "Meet the rules without reciting them" in Part B). The one kind of rule that is never answered in the pitch is a rule about commercial links, sponsorship, affiliate content or companies: see "Links and commercial status" in Part B. The pitch makes no statement about it, in either direction.
6. **Search fully before deciding there is no invitation.** Real guest-post pages are often unlinked or misnamed, so work through every step of Step 1 first. If the site loaded and the full search finds no invitation, the verdict is SKIP_NO_INVITATION, and the search log must show exactly what was tried. If fetch failures stopped the search (robots.txt block, JavaScript-only pages, repeated errors), the verdict is NEEDS_REVIEW instead, because the invitation may exist on a page that could not be read.
7. **Never pitch Indian or Pakistani websites.** Kai has decided that sites based in or run from India or Pakistan are excluded, whatever their quality, metrics or guest-post invitation. They are skipped at the very first gate (G0, SKIP_REGION_EXCLUDED) before any other work is done on them: no pitch route search, no angle, no pitch, no outreach draft, no Doc section. See Step 0.

## Inputs

- Leads normally arrive as a CSV file Kai uploads to the project (look in the message's attachment path or /mnt/project-files/uploads/hearth/, where files have no extension; check with `file`). Read it with a script that prints the header and a few rows, find the domain or URL column and any opportunity URL or score columns, normalize each to a bare domain, and drop rows already in the tracker ("Guest Post Master Tracker", including its "Full Articles" tab) so no site is redone. Record the CSV in /mnt/project-files/.notes/inputs.md. Split the remaining domains into batches of about 25, named by source and number in the tracker's batch column (for example C1-B1 for the first CSV's first batch). Google Sheets of leads, or pasted domains, are handled the same way.
- One or more domains. An `opportunity_url` may come with each (from a discovery tool). Treat it as an unverified hint only. Discovery tools often return a guessed URL (/guest-post, /contribute) or the wrong page (an /advertise page, a registry search page, a forum index, a closed application page, a gift list). The same domain may appear in several rows with different guessed URLs: evaluate each domain once and record every supplied URL.
- The pitch context. Default is Hotel Hot Tubs (hotelhottubs.com), see Part B for the author profile. If the user supplies a different context, use that.
